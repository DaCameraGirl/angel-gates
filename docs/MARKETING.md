# Marketing Plan

Angel Gates should lead with loss prevention and accountability, not gadget convenience.

## Positioning

Angel Gates is an edge-first gate access and evidence layer for properties that already have gate hardware but lack reliable accountability.

Core message:

```text
Every gate event is tied to a credential, timestamp, relay action, and video evidence window, so managers can recover damaged-arm costs instead of eating them.
```

QR codes, PINs, offline access, and the resident app are supporting features. The wedge is damaged-arm evidence and chargeback recovery.

## First Buyer

Start with properties where one person feels the gate pain directly:

- Self-storage facilities.
- Small HOAs and private roads.
- Apartment communities with older barrier arms and recurring damage.
- Regional property managers with multiple similar properties.

Avoid leading with large corporate multifamily portfolios until one live property has numbers. The first sale should be simple: one property, one gate, one manager, one maintenance contact, one case study.

## Pain To Quantify

Before a sales claim becomes public copy, validate:

- Annual spend on gate arms.
- Number of arm replacements in the last 12 months.
- Average cost per arm replacement.
- Labor cost or vendor cost per repair visit.
- How often the gate is left open after damage.
- Whether residents or visitors are ever charged back today.
- Monthly phone line or cellular cost for legacy dial-pad systems.
- Number of resident complaints tied to the gate.

Working hypothesis for the first property: recurring arm damage and open-gate periods cost roughly $4,000 to $8,000 per year. Treat that as a number to prove, not a number to advertise before records confirm it.

## Pitch

Use a short, operations-focused pitch:

```text
Your gate is already there. Angel Gates does not replace the operator or touch safety equipment. We add a local controller that authorizes QR/PIN access, logs every entry, pulses the existing open input, and attaches video evidence to events. When an arm is damaged, the manager can pull the exact time window and charge back the responsible unit or visitor.
```

For a 270-unit, one-gate property:

```text
Standard year-one price: about $4,197.
Ongoing annual SaaS: about $3,348.
If the property is losing $4,000 to $8,000/year on damaged arms and open-gate periods, the system can pay for itself in year one.
```

## Proof Points

The pilot should produce proof that can close the second property:

- Before/after gate-arm damage spend.
- Number of chargeback opportunities created.
- Number of entries with credential attribution.
- Number of denied or suspicious attempts captured.
- Manager quote about finding the right event window.
- Installer quote that safety devices and operator behavior were not changed.
- Screenshot of live event feed.
- Example incident packet with credential, relay event, timestamp, and video window.

Do not publish resident names, plate numbers, credential values, unit numbers, or raw incident video without written permission.

## Offer

Pilot offer:

- 90 days free.
- One gate.
- QR/PIN access.
- Camera-backed event evidence.
- Existing dial pad remains as fallback.
- Standard price named in the agreement.
- Conversion to paid monthly SaaS if success metrics are met.
- Permission to use anonymized metrics and a public reference if the property approves.

Standard production price:

- $499 hardware kit.
- $350 installation/setup.
- $279/month for a 270-unit, one-gate property.

## Outreach Path

1. Start with the maintenance contact: confirm arm damage frequency, repair costs, and cabinet/gate setup.
2. Meet the property manager: lead with the damaged-arm evidence workflow and the cost comparison.
3. Get permission for gate photos: cabinet, pedestal, lane, camera angle, power/network options.
4. Offer the 90-day pilot: written agreement, no safety modifications, fallback dial pad remains.
5. Run the pilot: collect metrics weekly, not only at the end.
6. Convert: use validated savings and manager quote to move to paid.
7. Ask for regional intro: show numbers, not promises.

## What Not To Lead With

- Do not lead with "resident app."
- Do not lead with "modern QR access."
- Do not lead with "AI" or "computer vision."
- Do not pitch replacing DoorKing, LiftMaster, Linear, or the gate operator.
- Do not claim certified safety compliance from this repository.
- Do not advertise the $4,000 to $8,000 savings range until the first property validates it.

Lead with the manager's loss:

```text
Damaged arms are costing money, and today there is no reliable evidence trail to recover it.
```

Then show how Angel Gates creates that evidence trail.
