# Competition

Angel Gates is not entering an empty category. The field already includes gate-access apps, HOA visitor-management platforms, broad access-control systems, OEM gate ecosystems, and local installer businesses.

The positioning should be honest:

```text
Angel Gates is narrower than the broad access-control platforms and more evidence-focused than the gate-app products. It is built for properties with existing barrier-arm gates that need local reliability, audit integrity, and damaged-arm chargeback proof without replacing the operator.
```

## Competitive Map

### Gatewise

Gatewise is a direct product comparison for app-based gate access. Its public positioning centers on multifamily access control, resident mobile access, management portals, digital wallet access, virtual call box workflows, self-guided tours, and integrations with property-management systems.

Where Gatewise is strong:

- Clear resident-facing mobile access story.
- Property-manager portal and portfolio visibility.
- Multifamily-specific sales language.
- Integrations with property-management systems.
- Fast installation and migration messaging.

Where Angel Gates should differ:

- Lead with chargeback evidence and damaged-arm loss prevention, not only convenience.
- Make local edge authorization the default so gates keep working when cloud connectivity fails.
- Treat tamper-evident event logs as core product infrastructure.
- Keep the retrofit story tied to the existing operator input instead of making the app the whole product story.

Do not position Gatewise as weak. Position Angel Gates as choosing a sharper first wedge: barrier-arm accountability and local authorization.

### Proptia

Proptia is a broad gated-community and HOA platform. Its public materials cover visitor management, license-plate recognition, Bluetooth/mobile access, gate call units, visitor kiosks, intercoms, virtual guarding, access-control payments, integrations, onboarding, and dealer installation.

Where Proptia is strong:

- Full gated-community operating platform.
- Visitor processing, gatehouse workflows, and virtual guard options.
- LPR and access-control modules already in market.
- Dealer and onboarding story.
- Good fit for communities that want a broad replacement platform.

Where Angel Gates should differ:

- Start smaller and more specific: one gate, existing operator, chargeback evidence, edge-first decisions.
- Avoid becoming a full HOA operations suite before the gate evidence workflow is proven.
- Win properties that do not want a broad platform migration but do want accountability at the barrier arm.
- Make the controller and event log the load-bearing product, not a module inside a larger community system.

Proptia is likely to show up in investor conversations. The answer is not "they do not matter." The answer is "they are broad; Angel Gates is focused."

### ButterflyMX

ButterflyMX is strong in property access and video intercom. Its public product set includes video intercoms, QR code intercom, access control, package rooms, security cameras, keypads, vehicle access control, smart locks, elevator controls, self-guided tours, and front desk tools.

Where ButterflyMX is strong:

- Recognized multifamily brand.
- Polished resident and visitor experience.
- Hardware and software platform breadth.
- Video intercom as a first-class workflow.
- Strong fit for properties replacing front-door or building-entry experiences.

Where Angel Gates should differ:

- Do not compete as a video-intercom company first.
- Focus on existing barrier-arm properties where the pain is damaged arms, open gates, old keypads, and missing evidence.
- Use video as evidence attached to gate events, not as the whole product category.
- Keep pilot scope away from intercom/video-call complexity until chargeback proof is working.

ButterflyMX can be a future integration or adjacent competitor. For the first property, it is mostly a reminder not to let the product drift into "build every access product."

### Brivo, PDK, And Broad Access-Control Platforms

Brivo and PDK-style platforms compete from the broader physical access-control market. They are built around cloud access control, control panels, readers, credentials, video, visitor management, integrations, and enterprise or multi-site operations.

Where broad platforms are strong:

- Mature access-control infrastructure.
- Dealer/reseller channels.
- Multi-door and multi-site administration.
- Compliance, enterprise security, and integrations.
- Hardware ecosystem depth.

Where Angel Gates should differ:

- Stay gate-retrofit specific instead of trying to secure every door in a portfolio.
- Treat barrier-arm event evidence as a product category, not a side report.
- Make the one-gate pilot cheap, fast, and easy to understand.
- Keep local authorization and relay pulse behavior visible in the product story.

These platforms are not wrong for enterprise buyers. Angel Gates should avoid enterprise platform sprawl until the first wedge is proven.

### OEM Ecosystems: DoorKing, LiftMaster, Linear/Nice

DoorKing, LiftMaster, Linear/Nice, and similar vendors are the default hardware ecosystem at many properties. They sell the operators, access-control accessories, entry systems, cloud tools, apps, replacement parts, and installer support that properties already know.

Where OEMs are strong:

- Installed base.
- Installer familiarity.
- Certified hardware and safety documentation.
- Direct fit with their own operators and accessories.
- Default vendor trust for repairs and replacements.

Where Angel Gates should differ:

- Do not pitch "replace DoorKing" or "replace LiftMaster."
- Pitch "keep your operator and safety equipment; add an authorization and evidence layer."
- Integrate through dry contact / relay input first.
- Make OEM cloud integrations optional future upgrades, not requirements for core compatibility.

OEMs are ecosystem anchors, not just competitors. The safest sales posture is to complement the operator, not challenge it.

### Local Gate Installers

Local gate and access-control installers are the most important non-software competitor and the most important channel.

Where installers are strong:

- They already have the property relationship.
- They know the cabinets, loops, operators, and failure patterns.
- Property managers trust them when the gate is broken.
- They can sell replacement keypads, telephone-entry hardware, cameras, and service plans.

How Angel Gates should work with them:

- Treat installers as deployment partners, not obstacles.
- Pay for qualified installation instead of making it an informal favor.
- Give them a clean commissioning flow and a margin on hardware/install.
- Keep the safety boundary clear: Angel Gates authorizes and pulses; the certified operator controls motion and safety.

If an installer can make money installing Angel Gates, they become distribution. If Angel Gates makes them look replaceable, they become resistance.

### Status Quo

The biggest competitor is often doing nothing:

- Keep the old dial pad.
- Leave the gate open after repeated damage.
- Replace arms without knowing who hit them.
- Keep using shared PINs that never rotate.
- Accept resident complaints as normal.

The sales case must make the status quo expensive. That requires field numbers:

- Arms replaced in the last 12 months.
- Cost per arm.
- Downtime after damage.
- Whether chargebacks happen today.
- Monthly dial-pad phone or cellular cost.
- Work orders tied to the gate.

Without those numbers, Angel Gates sounds like a nicer gate app. With those numbers, it becomes loss prevention.

## Positioning Statement

Use this when asked "why you, why now, and why not them?"

```text
Most access products lead with resident convenience or broad access-control scope. Angel Gates leads with local reliability and evidence. For properties with existing barrier-arm gates, we add an edge controller that authorizes QR/PIN access locally, pulses the existing operator input, and records each decision with relay and video evidence. The goal is not to replace the operator or become a full access-control suite on day one. The goal is to stop properties from eating damaged-arm costs without proof.
```

## Sales Posture

Say:

- "We work with the operator already in the cabinet."
- "We are an authorization and evidence layer."
- "The gate keeps working from local credentials when internet is down."
- "Every access decision creates an event record."
- "Video is tied to the event window for chargeback review."
- "The installer remains part of the deployment."

Do not say:

- "Nobody else is doing gate access."
- "We replace DoorKing, LiftMaster, or the installer."
- "We are certified access-control hardware."
- "This is only a cheaper gate app."
- "The camera is optional for the loss-prevention pitch."

## Investor Answer

If an investor asks about competition, the sober answer is:

```text
There are strong companies in gate access and broader access control: Gatewise, Proptia, ButterflyMX, Brivo, PDK-style platforms, OEM ecosystems, and local installers. Angel Gates is not trying to out-platform them at launch. The first wedge is narrower: existing barrier-arm properties with recurring arm damage and weak audit evidence. We win by combining local edge authorization, tamper-evident logs, relay-level retrofit compatibility, and event-linked video evidence for chargebacks.
```

The proof is not a feature checklist. The proof is a live property where the manager can say:

```text
Before Angel Gates, we replaced arms and ate the cost. After Angel Gates, we could identify the event window and recover the damage.
```
