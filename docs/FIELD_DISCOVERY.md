# Field Discovery Questions

Use this before pitching the first property. The goal is to turn assumptions about gate damage, open-gate periods, dial-pad cost, and decision authority into confirmed numbers.

Ask casually first. Do not start by asking for formal records.

## Maintenance Contact Questions

1. **How many gate arms have we replaced in the last year, ballpark?**

   Capture the off-the-cuff number. If the answer is "about one a month" or "more in summer," that is already useful.

2. **What does a new arm cost the property?**

   Confirm or correct the working `$180` arm-cost assumption. Ask whether pricing changes for rush orders or vendor calls.

3. **How long does the gate usually stay broken or left open when the arm goes down?**

   This is the open-gate liability and resident-confidence number. Capture hours or days, not just "a while."

4. **Do we ever charge anyone for the damage?**

   If the answer is "no, we just eat it," write that down exactly. That sentence is central to the chargeback pitch.

5. **How much does the phone line or cellular adapter for the dial pad cost each month?**

   This is a direct replacement-cost number. It helps show savings beyond damaged arms.

6. **Who actually decides on gate work: the manager, regional, B&H corporate, or somebody else?**

   This tells whether the pilot pitch goes through the property manager or needs a regional sponsor.

7. **Can I see a year of work orders for the gate when you have a minute?**

   Ask for records only after the conversation is warm. Work orders turn the sales hypothesis into confirmed evidence.

8. **Would you be willing to install the box when the hardware arrives if I pay you for the install?**

   Do not make this a favor. A paid installer creates a cleaner liability story and can become the first installer-channel reference.

## Bonus Question

Ask what is annoying or broken about the gate from the maintenance side.

Sometimes the answer is not a product feature. It may be useful pitch language, a wiring constraint, a vendor problem, or a clue about who really owns the decision.

## Notes To Capture

- Arm replacements in last 12 months.
- Cost per arm.
- Vendor/service-call cost per incident.
- Time gate stays broken or open after damage.
- Whether chargebacks happen today.
- Dial-pad phone/cellular monthly cost.
- Who approves gate-related spending.
- Whether work orders exist and who can share them.
- Whether the maintenance contact is willing to be paid installer for the pilot.

## Why This Matters

The current sales hypothesis is that damaged arms, open-gate periods, and dial-pad maintenance may cost a property roughly `$4,000` to `$8,000` per year.

Do not use that as public sales copy until field discovery confirms it. The first property conversation should produce the numbers that make the pitch defensible.
